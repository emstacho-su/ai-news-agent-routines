### [UPDATE] MCP RC: Streamable HTTP routing headers and TTL caching for list responses

**Background:** MCP RC locked May 21; stateless core removes session-level state and the initialize/initialized handshake.

**New technical details from the RC:** Streamable HTTP requests must now carry `Mcp-Method` and `Mcp-Name` headers, enabling load balancers and API gateways to route on operation type without body inspection — a concrete fix for the session-affinity problem in production deployments. Additionally, `tools/list`, `resources/list`, and resource-read responses now carry `ttlMs` and `cacheScope` fields (modeled on HTTP Cache-Control), giving clients a defined staleness budget so they can avoid re-fetching tool manifests on every turn. 10-week SDK validation window is open; final specification publishes July 28, 2026.

Sources: [MCP Blog RC post](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/), [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/model-context-protocol-roadmap-2026/)

## Research & Technical

### [NEW] OpenAI reasoning model disproves Erdős's 80-year unit distance conjecture

An OpenAI internal general-purpose reasoning model — not a math-specific system and not scaffolded to search for proofs — disproved Paul Erdős's unit distance conjecture in discrete geometry, one of his favorite open problems since 1946. The conjecture asks: given _n_ points in the plane, what is the maximum number of pairs at exactly distance 1? For ~80 years, square grids were believed to be essentially optimal. The model found an infinite family of constructions yielding a polynomial improvement, proving the conjecture false.

The proof was independently checked by Noga Alon (Princeton), Melanie Wood, and Thomas Bloom — the same Bloom who previously disproved a high-profile claimed proof in this area — all providing supportive statements. OpenAI has published the result. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/openai-claims-it-solved-an-80-year-old-math-problem-for-real-this-time/), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48212493)

**Why it matters:** A general reasoning model, not fine-tuned for math, making a genuine novel mathematical discovery (not just verification) is a meaningful capability signal. If this result holds up to further scrutiny, it's the clearest evidence yet that frontier reasoning models can contribute to open research problems, not just assist with known ones.

## Wildcard

### [NEW] Trump pulls planned AI executive order on voluntary pre-release model sharing

The White House canceled a planned executive order that would have required leading AI companies to voluntarily share advanced frontier models with the government for up to 90 days of national security review before public release. The EO had been negotiated with OpenAI, Anthropic, and other labs; a signing ceremony was already scheduled. Trump told reporters he "didn't like certain aspects of it" and cited concern about interfering with America's lead over China. The order had been postponed multiple times before this final cancellation. Whether it gets renegotiated or dropped entirely is unclear.

This is distinct from the existing CAISI pre-deployment evaluation framework (which covers security testing at NIST and has labs already participating voluntarily). The proposed EO would have created a broader 90-day pre-release gate with White House oversight. Sources: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/21/trump-ai-executive-order-postponed-why), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/21/trump-ai-executive-order-postponed.html), [WaPo](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/05/21/white-house-tore-down-ai-rules-now-its-building-new-defenses/)
