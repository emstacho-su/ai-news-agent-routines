## Anthropic

### [NEW] Mythos 5 redesigns quantum circuits autonomously: 17x gate reduction in elliptic curve operations

Anthropic published research (Oct 16, in collaboration with Harvard) reporting that Claude Mythos 5 worked autonomously for approximately 53 hours to redesign eight foundational quantum circuit tasks spanning chemistry, optimization, physics simulation, and number theory. Seven of the eight designs reduced both gate count and qubit requirements versus the study's baselines.

The headline result is the elliptic curve point addition circuit — foundational to breaking ECDSA and ECDH: Toffoli gate count dropped from approximately 5.1 million to approximately 440,000 (a ~17x reduction), at the cost of 12% more logical qubits (1,837 vs 1,938). Anthropic's Quantum STEM Fellows program delivered the eight tasks as pure text descriptions paired with an automated scoring system. Results were validated through computational resource estimates, not runs on actual quantum hardware; practical benefits depend on integration into complete fault-tolerant algorithms.

Sources: [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/17/anthropics-claude-designs-leaner-quantum-circuits-reducing-resources-needed-for-key-calculations/), [QuantumZeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/claude-quantum-optimization-anthropics-improves/), [Xenospectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/claude-quantum-circuit-history-recovery/).

**Why it matters:** Elliptic curve point addition is the innermost loop for breaking ECC — HTTPS, SSH, code-signing certificates all depend on it. A 17x gate reduction compresses the compute requirements for a future cryptographically-relevant quantum attack. It also signals a pattern: Mythos-class models are now materially contributing to quantum algorithm research, not just security scanning.

### [UPDATE] METR embedded access: operational details of three-tier evaluator structure

**Background:** Anthropic committed in September to embed independent safety evaluators with employee-level access. AEF-1 was cosigned by Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI (Sept 15); Accenture's Faculty unit named first embedded evaluator (Sept 18). Reuters (Oct 13) reported both labs sought editorial review rights over evaluator publications — in direct tension with Amodei's Sept 12 pledge of no editorial control.

**New:** Anthropic's Oct 15 post clarified its evaluator program has three access tiers. SecureBio is Tier 1 (lowest). METR holds Tier 3 (highest, most expansive access). The METR team consists of six researchers who receive company hardware, office space, and internal tooling comparable to Anthropic's own risk assessment teams. Customer data, privileged legal information, and model architecture are off-limits. Critically: Anthropic cannot veto METR's publications; it may require redactions only in a narrow set of predefined categories, and METR may publicly state whether it found those redactions justified. As of the announcement, METR has published no evaluation results from this access. Independence critics note the labs still select their own reviewers.

Sources: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/an-update-on-independent-evaluators), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/anthropic-metr-securebio-ai-safety-evaluation/), [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/ai-embedded-evaluator-jobs/).

## Research & Technical

### [UPDATE] DeepMind Gemini 4 Argon Safety Report: no AI acceleration detected; 75% code AI-written

**Background:** Gemini 4 Argon launched broadly Oct 13 at introductory pricing of $2/$10/MTok, aimed at long-horizon software engineering, legal, and cybersecurity tasks. DeepMind CEO Hassabis stepped down Aug 2026; Koray Kavukcuoglu is now CEO.

**New:** DeepMind's Gemini 4 Argon Safety & Security Report (Oct 14) reports no statistically significant acceleration in the pace of AI research progress. The team used GCI v1, an internal index aggregating scores of 123 AI models across 92 benchmarks into a single monthly value. Frontier capability gains averaged 0.091 GCI points/month before April 2026 and 0.118 points/month after — no significant structural difference. Gemini 4 Argon landed slightly above trend, which the report attributes to additional compute and organizational investment, not self-acceleration.

Additional findings: AI tools now generate more than 75% of production code at DeepMind, but 88% of code changes still require human review. Fully autonomous code deployments rose from 7.5% to 11.9%. Gemini 4 Argon solved roughly 50% of internal research engineering tasks — well below the 90% threshold DeepMind designates as a red flag for full automation risk. Survey of 12 senior researchers: automation works on clearly verifiable tasks, struggles with open-ended ideation.

Sources: [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/381061/google-deepmind-ai-isnt-building-itself-writes-most-code), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/google-deepmind-no-ai-research-acceleration/), [Digital Today KR](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/115695/google-deepmind-says-no-evidence-ai-self-improvement-acceleration-gap-with-industry-warnings).

**Why it matters:** DeepMind's measured GCI evidence directly diverges from Anthropic's own Frontier Red Team warning (Oct 14: "small models are months from autonomous IoT self-replication"). The GCI methodology isn't independently validated, but it's DeepMind's formal evidence position. When two top labs produce contradictory empirical claims about the pace of progress, that disagreement itself is the story — and it will drive regulatory framing.

## Model Releases

### [NEW] Meta Muse Spark Open: 816B-parameter open-weight MoE for coding and agents

Meta released Muse Spark Open (mid-October 2026), an open-weights release of its Muse Spark model architecture. Specs: 816B total parameters, ~42B active per token (mixture-of-experts), 1M-token context window, natively multimodal (text, image, video). Weights are on Hugging Face under Meta's proprietary "Muse Model License" — not an OSI-approved open-source license; commercial use terms apply. Hosted API access is available through OpenRouter, Groq, and major cloud marketplaces (AWS SageMaker, Google Cloud, Azure Foundry). Hardware requirements: minimum 700GB–1TB GPU memory; Meta positions a dual Mac Studio M5 Ultra setup as a desktop option. All benchmarks are self-reported by Meta; independent evaluation is pending. Note that Muse Spark Open shares its base architecture with Muse Spark 1.3 but is a distinct open-weights release, separate from the Muse Wonder/Wonder Full frontier series.

Sources: [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/meta-muse-spark-open/), [DeepLearning.AI](https://charonhub.deeplearning.ai/with-muse-spark-meta-pivots-away-from-its-open-weights-llama-strategy/), [Trending Topics EU](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/muse-spark-open-meta-open-weight-license/), [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/meta-releases-open-weight-ai-model-muse-spark-open-for-mac).

**Why it matters:** This is Meta's first open-weight release at the 800B+ parameter scale, pushing back into a space it ceded when the Llama line maxed out at 405B. The "Muse Model License" (not MIT or Apache) is a material distinction — check it before deploying commercially. At 42B active parameters in MoE, the inference profile is closer to a 42B dense model; the 700GB+ VRAM requirement is for the full-weight checkpoint, not inference.

## Practical Tips

- **Quantum circuits and cryptography timelines:** The Mythos 5 result doesn't mean ECC is broken now — it still requires fault-tolerant quantum hardware at scale. But it narrows the algorithmic overhead for such an attack. If you're planning any service with long-lived cryptographic key material (multi-year data retention, code-signing certs), the NIST PQC standardized algorithms (ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA) are now worth testing in your stack.
- **Meta Muse Spark Open weights:** If you want to evaluate without a cloud bill, Hugging Face has the weights. Be aware the Muse Model License requires a Meta commercial license for any service with >100M monthly active users — unlikely to apply but worth reading page 2.
