## Policy & Regulation

### [NEW] DHS bans DeepSeek from all federal civilian agency IT systems

The Department of Homeland Security issued an order invoking the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018 (FASCSA) to permanently ban DeepSeek from federal civilian agency IT infrastructure. The Federal Register notice (docket DHS-2026-0628) was published October 20.

The order covers three threat vectors cited by DHS: (1) transfer of sensitive federal data to PRC-controlled servers embedded in DeepSeek's API, (2) intelligence-collection risk against US persons and agency employees, and (3) potential malware or surveillance mechanisms in the model weights themselves. Critically, the ban extends to **third-party software products and services built on DeepSeek implementations** — not just direct DeepSeek API use — though DHS did not publish a specific list of covered products.

The order does not extend to private companies.

Sources: [Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/102669-dhs-banning-deepseek-from-us-federal-civilian-agencies), [Bank InfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/dhs-bans-deepseek-from-federal-civilian-agencies-a-33146), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/dhs-bans-deepseek-across-federal-government-networks/), [TFTC](https://www.tftc.io/deepseek-dhs-exclusion-removal-order-civilian-federal-it), [Mad Robot](https://madrobot.blog/2026/10/19/us-bans-deepseek-federal-agencies-dhs-order-remove-third-party/)

**Why it matters:** The FASCSA route is significant — it's the same statutory authority used to ban Huawei and ZTE hardware. The "third-party products built on DeepSeek" language is broad enough to sweep in open-source derivatives, which creates real compliance headaches for anyone who integrated DeepSeek weights into an enterprise tool that reaches federal customers.

## Research & Technical

### [NEW] AlphaGo co-creator Thore Graepel raises tens of millions for reasoning AI startup Metis Reasoning

Thore Graepel — a co-creator of AlphaGo and longtime Google DeepMind principal researcher — left DeepMind this summer and is raising an initial round in the tens of millions (with a larger tranche in the hundreds of millions under discussion) for Metis Reasoning, a London-based AI startup. First reported by Bloomberg on Sept. 24.

Metis Reasoning's stated focus: AI systems that can handle unfamiliar problems and choose actions under uncertainty — explicitly framed as bringing AlphaGo-style reasoning (plan, search, evaluate) to frontier models rather than continuing to scale autoregressive LLMs. Target domains include robotics, science, and engineering. The startup's architecture and commercial model have not been disclosed.

The founding follows David Silver's exit to Ineffable Intelligence (Jan. 2026) and Graepel's departure from Alphabet. Graepel previously spent 2021–2025 at Altos Labs working on biology-adjacent AI before returning to DeepMind.

Sources: [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/alphago-co-creator-thore-graepel-raises-tens-of-millions-for-ai-reasoning), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=4229172), [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/deepmind-graepel-ai-reasoning-startup/), [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/thore-graepel-ai-reasoning-startup-fundraise)

**Why it matters:** Graepel's departure is part of a broader DeepMind research alumni pattern (Silver, Graepel, and others all founding separate shops). Metis Reasoning is the most directly compute-efficiency-oriented play in this cohort — a bet that AlphaGo-style search and planning can outperform raw scaling.
