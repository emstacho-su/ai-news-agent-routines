## Anthropic

### [UPDATE] Fable 5.2 cuts math autoformalization from 11 days to ~30 hours

**Background:** In September 2026, Claude produced the first fully computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 11 days. The November 9 Fable 5.2 release cited ongoing math research as a key capability area.

**New:** Quanta Magazine reports that Anthropic researchers announced on November 9 that the same autoformalization workflow now takes approximately 30 hours — a ~9x speedup. The improvement is attributed to Fable 5.2's faster token generation and improved formal-math reasoning. As before, the results are verified computationally, not by hardware. No new unsolved problem was claimed solved; this is a process speedup on the existing formalization pipeline.

**Why it matters:** If the 30-hour number holds across different proof structures, AI-assisted formal verification starts to fit inside a weekend experiment rather than a week-long compute run — a meaningful threshold for adoption in math and software verification toolchains.

Sources: [Quanta Magazine](https://www.quantamagazine.org/update/169537/), [TechRadar on 11-day formalization](https://techradar.com/pro/anthropic-formalizes-fermats-last-theorem-like-never-before-using-claude-but-it-still-took-11-days-to-write-out)

## Tools & Frameworks

### [NEW] Claude Code 2.1.329: `/tasks` lists scheduled tasks and forks

Released approximately November 19, 2026. Changes per third-party tracker Havoptic (official changelog not yet updated at briefing time):

- `/tasks` now shows all scheduled tasks, `/loop` wakeups, and running forks in one place — previously you had to infer fork and schedule state from session context.
- `/status` flags marketplace installations that lack auto-update support, surfacing stale plugin versions.
- Fixes: self-hosted runner sessions incorrectly shown as stopped while still starting; Code Mode retaining oversized text files; `/teleport` and sign-out edge cases.

The release follows 2.1.328 (auto mode on Bedrock/Vertex/Azure) by one day. No breaking changes noted.

**Why it matters:** `/tasks` visibility is directly relevant to this project — a quick way to audit what Routines, loops, and forks are live in a session without reading through session history.

Sources: [Havoptic 2.1.329 tracker](https://www.havoptic.com/r/claude-code-2.1.329), [gradually.ai Claude Code changelog](https://www.gradually.ai/en/changelogs/claude-code/)

## Research & Technical

### [NEW] OpenAI + AMD: AI-redesigned GPU circuits find up to 14% PPA gains

Announced November 18, 2026. OpenAI reports its models rewrote the SystemVerilog RTL (register-transfer level) code that describes circuitry in AMD's flagship GPU, using Synopsys EDA tools to verify behavioral equivalence at each rewrite step. Headline result: up to 14% power-performance-area improvement on individual circuit blocks, compared with already-optimized designs, in under five months.

Key caveats:
- 14% applies to specific blocks, not the whole GPU end-to-end. The claim is PPA gain, not raw compute throughput on a shipping chip.
- Results are self-reported by OpenAI; AMD did not independently publish benchmark data or identify the specific GPU.
- AMD reportedly incorporated some changes into a design already sent for manufacturing and added others to future production files. Separately, single-digit PPA gains were claimed on a next-gen AMD CPU block.
- No public design files or third-party audit.

For context: OpenAI's Jalapeño ASIC (covered earlier) was described as "partly designed using AI models." This AMD collaboration is the first time a PPA number has been put to that claim at GPU scale, with a named EDA partner.

Sources: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-models-amd-gpu-chip-design-14-percent), [Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news/openai-and-amd-achieve-a-year-of-chip-design-progress-in-under-five-months), [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/openai-s-models-rewrote-amd-s-gpu-circuits-and-gained-14-in-five-months)

## Wildcard

### [NEW] House FA Committee: Commerce approved ~879K AI chips for Chinese firms

Chair Brian Mast (House Foreign Affairs) disclosed November 19, 2026 — using Export Control Reform Act authority — that the Commerce Department approved export licenses for approximately 879,000 Nvidia H200 and equivalent AMD AI chips to Chinese companies between December 2025 and May 2026, out of 3.4 million requested.

Named recipients: Alibaba (~75K), Tencent (~150K), Baidu (~43K), BYD (~4K). The committee also flagged two firms — Maginfra and New Matrix — as suspected smuggling fronts with empty offices. All four Chinese companies named in the report appear on the Pentagon's list of alleged Chinese military-backed companies.

One important caveat: the committee disclosed license approvals, not confirmed shipments — it's unclear how many chips physically reached China.

This is distinct from two overlapping stories: (1) the BIS interim final rule extending export controls to overseas remote access (covered Nov 18), and (2) the ~600K Nvidia H200 stockpile in Hong Kong blocked by Beijing's import hold (covered Nov 20). All three are part of the same chip-control picture but represent different choke points (US export approval, Beijing import approval, and now the Congressional scrutiny layer).
