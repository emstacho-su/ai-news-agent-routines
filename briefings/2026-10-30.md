## Anthropic

### [UPDATE] Public S-1 Filed Oct 26: $16.3B H1 Revenue, Nasdaq Ticker "ANT"

Anthropic filed its public Form S-1 with the SEC on October 26, 2026 to list Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker **ANT**. The filing discloses approximately **$16.3 billion in revenue** for the first half of 2026, with a reported H1 operating loss around $693 million (one aggregator cited an $80B figure — likely cumulative/total-cost variant, not operating loss; treat with caution until primary filing is verified). Share count and price range were left blank; a November listing window is widely cited but Anthropic has not fixed a date. The filing follows the confidential draft S-1 filed June 1, 2026, and the $65B Series H that put valuation at $965B.

## Model Releases

### [UPDATE] Mistral Large 4 Open Weights: October 31 Target, Custom License

Mistral's 1T-parameter "Le Chonk" model — in public preview since October 6 — has its open weights targeting **October 31** per the Hugging Face listing. (Some coverage cited October 27; that date was from secondary outlets, not Mistral directly. The company only committed to "end of October.") Key details now confirmed: 49B active parameters per token (MoE), natively multimodal, trained with a two-stage pipeline (SFT + RL). The RL stage is **still ongoing** — Mistral says further gains are expected over coming weeks. License: custom Mistral license, not Apache 2.0 as with Large 3. Self-reported active param count also differs between launch post (49B) and the model page (52B) — unresolved.

**Sources:** [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/mistrals-1t-parameter-large-4-slated-for-october-31-drop), [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/mistral-large-4-preview-open-weights/), [BornCity (DE)](https://borncity.com/news/mistral-large-4-modellgewichte-sollen-am-27-oktober-2026-erscheinen/)

**Why it matters:** This is the first 1T-param MoE with downloadable weights (assuming it ships). The custom license and mid-RL release state matter for anyone planning to run it locally — usage terms are unclear until the weights land.

## Research & Technical

### [NEW] Chinese APT Groups Automate Multi-Target Exploitation with DeepSeek + Hermes Agent

Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 published findings (dated July 30, 2026) describing a Chinese-linked group using aliases **knaithe** and **KnYuan** that wired DeepSeek into the open-source **Hermes Agent** orchestration tool to fully automate the scan → exploit cycle. The pairing scanned more than 460 targets with minimal human input. In one recovered session dated May 7, 2026, DeepSeek autonomously narrowed 25,209 exposed n8n instances to three vulnerable targets. TeamT5 researchers separately report that Chinese state-affiliated groups more than doubled their attack volume after adopting AI tooling, with DeepSeek the most common choice — cited for capability, low cost, and weaker safety guardrails vs. Western models. A separate group, Grimfengxi, used DeepSeek for exploit code writing; Huapi used a Chinese model (likely DeepSeek) against a Taiwanese company's email system.

**Sources:** [Rankiteo Blog](https://blog.rankiteo.io/deenou1785515277-deepseek-hermes-agent-cyber-attack-july-2026/), [OODALOOP](https://oodaloop.com/briefs/cyber/chinas-hackers-use-deepseek-for-attacks-researchers-say/), [Implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/chinese-hackers-double-attack-volume-deepseek/)

**Why it matters:** The Hermes Agent + DeepSeek stack is reproducible and open-source. This is the first documented case of an agentic AI loop closing the scan-to-exploit gap at scale without human intermediation. Relevant to Stack's security concentration — this is exactly the threat model the defensive AI tooling discussed in recent briefings (Snyk Evo, NVIDIA Open Agent Safety Platform) is designed to counter.

## Wildcard

### [NEW] AI Data Center Backlash Hits the 2026 Midterms: 39–49 Ballot Measures on November 3

Ballotpedia is tracking **39–49 local ballot measures** on AI/data center construction across at least eight states (OH, IL, WI, KS, CA, MI, NV, FL), with 35–41 of them on the November 3, 2026 ballot. The measures are predominantly citizen-initiated (82% by petition). Ohio has the most measures, including proposals to ban data center construction in cities like Granville and Urbana. Early results: a Wisconsin city already **passed the nation's first anti-data-center referendum**; an LA-area city also passed a voter-approved ban. A May 2026 Gallup poll found 71% of Americans oppose data center construction in their community. Context: a bipartisan NPR report notes that AI power and water demands are the primary driver of opposition, not AI per se.

**Sources:** [Ballotpedia](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/21/ohio-leads-states-in-number-of-local-ballot-measures-on-data-centers-for-2026/), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/map-shows-states-where-data-centers-are-on-the-ballot-12528253), [EE News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/wisconsin-city-passes-nations-first-anti-data-center-referendum), [Design News](https://www.designnews.com/artificial-intelligence/this-tech-issue-is-reshaping-the-2026-midterm-elections)
