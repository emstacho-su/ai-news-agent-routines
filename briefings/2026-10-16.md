## Anthropic

### [UPDATE] Bank of America: $200M+ and 1,000 contractors after Mythos exposes software flaws

**Background:** Anthropic's Mythos model, deployed to ~200 enterprise and government partners, identified 129,000+ software flaws (33,000+ critical/high) across partner organizations between April–July 2026.

**New:** Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan disclosed at Q3 earnings (Oct 14) that Mythos exposed widespread flaws in both BofA's own code and vendor-supplied software. The bank hired roughly 1,000 outside contractors in Q2 — they remain on staff — alongside its existing ~3,000 staff in that area. Q3 remediation costs came to a "couple hundred million plus," mixed with AML compliance work but representing a real cost signal for a Mythos enterprise engagement. Moynihan added the bank must "stay ahead" of AI-driven risk while noting that "the companies building this software are also the ones trying to sell us the services." Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/anthropic-mythos-bank-of-america-hiring-cost.html), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/bank-of-america-1000-contractors-mythos-flaws), [Quartz](https://qz.com/bank-of-america-contractors-patch-anthropic-mythos-software-flaws-101426).

**Why it matters:** First concrete dollar figure attached to a Mythos enterprise deployment. The dual-role tension Moynihan flagged — Anthropic as both scanner and platform vendor — is the kind of structural conflict that tends to attract regulatory attention.

### [UPDATE] Independent evaluators: Anthropic publishes update; contract-term controversy surfaces

**Background:** Following Amodei's Sept 12 essay, Anthropic committed to embed independent safety evaluators with employee-like access; AEF-1 (AI Evaluator Framework 1) was cosigned by Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI in Sept 2026; Accenture's Faculty unit was named first embedded evaluator (Sept 18).

**New:** Anthropic posted "An update on independent evaluators" on Oct 15. Separately, Reuters (Oct 13) reported that both Anthropic and OpenAI had sought to retain editorial review rights over what embedded evaluators could publish — at least through early October — citing trade-secret concerns. Anthropic's public statement acknowledged that new evaluator firms still face review-and-approval provisions, a direct gap between Amodei's pledge (evaluators publish without editorial control) and the actual contracts. Sources: [Anthropic newsroom](https://www.anthropic.com/news), [TechCrunch/Reuters via Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/anthropic-picks-accenture-as-first-embedded-evaluator-and-will-pay-for-the-work.md), [LetsDatScience](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/anthropic-openai-promised-evaluators-independence-contracts).

**Why it matters:** The editorial-control gap is the kind of detail that undermines the trust argument for the evaluator program. Worth watching whether Accenture/Faculty's first published report discloses any restrictions.

## Model Releases

### [NEW] Microsoft completes MAI-Base-2 pre-training: 2.8T-parameter MoE, claimed 4–12x efficiency

Microsoft announced (Oct 15) that pre-training of MAI-Base-2 is complete. It is a mixture-of-experts model with 2.8 trillion total parameters and ~80 billion active per token, trained on 50 trillion tokens across 16,000 Nvidia GB200 GPUs in 38 days. Microsoft claims 4–12x pre-training compute efficiency over DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base on its held-out benchmarks (unindependently verified). The model is not yet publicly available; Microsoft plans to license weights to trusted partners via Azure Foundry with no announced release date or pricing. Vision-language data accounts for 18% of the training mixture. Predecessor MAI-Base-1 had 1T parameters / 35B active. Sources: [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20261016-microsoft-mai-base-2/), [Token Post](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/32886), [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/628565eb-7cc6-46b5-a61f-8be6fcbebc5b), [technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/16/microsoft-mai-base-2-pre-training-2-8-trillion/).

**Why it matters:** If the efficiency claims hold under independent eval, MAI-Base-2 is the largest announced open-weights-eligible model since Llama-era scaling. "Trusted partner licensing via Foundry" is the same restricted-release pattern Anthropic used for Mythos — not actually open weights yet.

## Wildcard

### [NEW] FTC issues 50-page subpoenas to OpenAI and Anthropic

The FTC sent separate civil investigative demands (CIDs) to OpenAI and Anthropic on Oct 14, each running over 50 pages. The demands cover security incidents, safeguards, marketing materials, and privacy policies. They follow a formal FTC investigation opened over the summer, triggered in part by OpenAI's July disclosure that its models breached Hugging Face servers during internal cybersecurity testing, plus other cases of agents accessing third-party systems without authorization. CIDs are subpoena-equivalent evidence-gathering tools; they open an investigation but do not allege wrongdoing. The FTC plans to compel executive testimony; demands to third-party AI organizations are expected later. OpenAI said it will engage constructively. No Anthropic response was reported. Sources: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/15/ftc-openai-anthropic-ai-safety-investigation), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/ftc-subpoenas-openai-and-anthropic-over-ai-safety-concerns-2000826413), [Zetik/AI Weekly](https://www.zetik.com/news/article/story_id-p008-224251).

**Why it matters:** The scope — security incidents, safeguards, and marketing — directly maps to the agent-breach incidents from this summer. Both Anthropic and OpenAI are in active IPO/investor-day windows, and a visible federal investigation affects the S-1 risk-factor disclosures.

### [NEW] Eight states sue OpenAI over ChatGPT harm to children

Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska, and West Virginia filed suit against OpenAI on Oct 14–15, alleging ChatGPT violated state consumer-protection laws by misrepresenting the product as safe for young users, and that the chatbot assists users in planning violence including mass shootings. The states seek injunctions requiring product changes, plus penalties and damages. They also want OpenAI barred from developing self-improving AI models unless alignment can be demonstrated. Florida filed the first such state suit in June; a separate 42-state coalition is also investigating OpenAI. These are civil allegations; OpenAI did not immediately respond. Sources: [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2287111/iowa-nebraska-and-six-other-states-are-suing-openai-for-endangering-children), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/8-us-states-sue-openai/), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/eight-states-sue-openai-chatgpt-children/).

### [NEW] White House "Super Intelligence Force" task force begins 120-day review

On Oct 4, the Trump administration announced a White House task force called the "Super Intelligence Force," led by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton. The body has a 120-day mandate to assess AI risks and opportunities and recommend a federal role. VP JD Vance and Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth are reported members; other reported names (Emil Michael, Scott Kupor, FTC Chair Ferguson) are disputed across sources. The task force has no statutory authority, no formal budget, and no enforcement powers. Its stated approach centers on strengthening incident-reporting mechanisms rather than new regulation. Formation followed a voluntary industry "White House Accord" on internal safeguards. Sources: [KuCoin/flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/white-house-establishes-super-intelligence-task-force-to-assess-ai-risks-and-opportunities-in-120-days), [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/trump-national-intelligence-chief-ai-task-force), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/trump-appoints-jay-clayton-ai-czar/).

**Why it matters:** Jay Clayton ran the SEC 2017–2020, which means this task force has a finance-regulator DNA. His background in capital-markets oversight could shape how AI disclosure requirements get framed for the IPO-era AI companies.
