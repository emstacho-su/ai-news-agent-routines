## Wildcard

### [NEW] Google DeepMind loses its CEO and chief scientist on the same day

Google announced on August 5–6 that Demis Hassabis is stepping down as CEO of Google DeepMind to become its Chair and Alphabet's first Chief Scientist. He will continue leading Isomorphic Labs, DeepMind's drug-discovery spinoff. Jeff Dean — 27-year Google veteran, co-creator of TensorFlow, and current Chief Scientist — is leaving to co-found **Discovery Loop**, a public benefit corporation where Google will serve as both investor and cloud provider. Joining Dean are three former Google legends: Oriol Vinyals (AlphaGo lead), Quoc Le (Transformer co-author), and Sanjay Ghemawat (Dean's longtime collaborator). Koray Kavukcuoglu, current CTO of DeepMind, becomes SVP and reports directly to Sundar Pichai. Alphabet shares dropped approximately 5% on the news.

**Context:** The reshuffling follows months of pressure — Gemini 3.5 Pro underperformed and shipped late, two Gemini co-leads quit the same week of the announcement, and competitors (Anthropic, OpenAI) have been closing the capability gap. DeepMind's AlphaFold team was already disbanded in July and reassigned to Gemini. Gemini 4 is confirmed in pre-training as of July 21, described by Pichai as "significantly larger than any prior Gemini." Kavukcuoglu now owns delivery.

**Why it matters:** Dean and Vinyals built foundational infrastructure Google still runs on. Discovery Loop's PBC structure (with Google as investor) is the first time a "Google spin-out" of this magnitude has operated explicitly as a public-benefit entity rather than a pure commercial lab. Likely to pursue ambitious basic-research bets Alphabet's Gemini roadmap couldn't accommodate. Worth watching for hiring signals and early papers.

### [UPDATE] OpenAI public S-1: mid-to-late August filing expected

**Background:** OpenAI filed a confidential S-1 with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley underwriting on June 8; $852B private valuation, ~$2B/month revenue but unprofitable at roughly -$1.22 per dollar earned.

**New:** As of August 9, the full document has not appeared on SEC EDGAR. Standard SEC review takes 60–90 days with multiple comment rounds; the public S-1 becomes available roughly 15 days before roadshow, putting earliest realistic public filing at mid-to-late August with a September listing window. Reuters reported in late June that a 2027 listing remains under active consideration. OpenAI's nonprofit board retains board-appointment control, meaning public shareholders won't have standard governance power.

## Research & Technical

### [NEW] DARPA VENOM: standard F-16 flies under AI control at Eglin AFB

DARPA disclosed on July 16 that a production F-16 — modified only with a bolt-on hardware and software kit under the Viper Experimentation and Next-generation Operations Model (VENOM) program — flew portions of combat-style missions under full AI autonomous control at Eglin Air Force Base. A human safety pilot is aboard to monitor, not to fly. The program moved through two phases: June 2026 saw piloted validation flights to verify the VENOM kit's hardware and software, and July saw the AI agent assume autonomous control of flight segments.

The key distinction from prior autonomous aviation demos (e.g., DARPA's earlier ACE program dogfight work): VENOM installs on a standard Air Force F-16 from the operational fleet. It's not a one-off research airframe — the intent is a scalable bolt-on that the service could deploy across its existing inventory.
