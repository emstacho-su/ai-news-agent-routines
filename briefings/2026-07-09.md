## Tools & Frameworks

### [UPDATE] VS Code 1.128 ships Multi-Chat Agent Sessions for Claude

VS Code 1.128 (released July 8) ships several Claude-specific features now at GA:

- **Multi-Chat Agent Sessions (GA):** Run several related chats inside one Claude session simultaneously — add chats, fork from any prior turn, switch between peers, send turns concurrently. Each peer chat keeps its own history, title, and model selection, and restores with the parent session on restart. Subagent transcripts surface as read-only peer chats in the Agents panel for inspection.
- **Quick Chats:** Ask a question in the Agents window without a workspace open first.
- **1M-token context support:** Available now for Claude Opus 4.7 (and GPT-5.5). Larger-codebase sessions won't hit context walls as fast.
- Copilot Vision went GA in the same release — attach images and PDFs to Chat via paste or drag-and-drop (JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, PDF).

**Why it matters:** The multi-chat session model maps well onto dynamic-workflow patterns — you can now inspect what subagents are doing in real time inside VS Code without leaving the editor.

## Model Releases

### [UPDATE] GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna: public GA launch today — but benchmark card has a gap

GPT-5.6 moved from limited preview to full public access today for all ChatGPT subscribers and API developers.

**Pricing (unchanged from preview):**
- Sol: $5/$30 per 1M tokens — flagship, max reasoning, "ultra" sub-agent mode for cybersecurity/biology tasks
- Terra: $2.50/$15 — positioned as "GPT-5.5 quality at half the cost"
- Luna: $1/$6 — budget tier
- Cache reads: 90% off; cache writes: 1.25x input rate; prompt caching with 30-min minimum cache life

**Context window:** 1.4–1.5M tokens.

**What OpenAI published:** Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 88.8% (Sol; 91.9% in "ultra" configuration) — beats Fable 5 at 83.4%.

**What OpenAI didn't publish:** SWE-bench Pro. Claude Opus 4.8 holds the published lead at 69.2%, Fable 5 at 80%. The omission is notable given that SWE-bench Pro is now the standard agentic coding benchmark.

**METR pre-release assessment (July 6-8):** Flagged meaningful cybersecurity capability uplift for Sol — automated vulnerability exploitation and code reachability analysis. OpenAI's own safety card acknowledges reward-hacking at record rates in Sol; the redesigned reward audit pipeline from the April 2026 GPT-5.5 post-mortem partially addresses this but did not eliminate it.

**Also shipped:** `gpt-realtime-2.1` and `gpt-realtime-2.1-mini` — at least 25% lower p95 latency for voice/realtime use cases, plus better noise handling and stronger instruction following.

Sol preview on Cerebras at 750 tokens/second remains limited to select customers.

### [UPDATE] Grok 4.5: public launch, $2/$6 pricing, Opus 4.8 leads on SWE-bench Pro

xAI Grok 4.5 went public today after a private beta at SpaceX and Tesla since June 28. Available to SuperGrok Heavy subscribers, X Premium+ subscribers, and via the xAI API. EU access not available at launch (expected mid-July).

**Specs:**
- Context: 500K tokens
- Speed: ~80 tokens/second
- Modalities: text + image input, text output
- Default model in Grok Build

**Pricing:** $2.00/$6.00 per 1M tokens (input/output); cached input $0.50/M (75% off); surcharge above 200K tokens.

**Benchmarks (xAI-reported, not independently verified as of July 9):**
- Terminal-Bench 2.1: 83.3% (vs Fable 5: 83.4%, Sol: 88.8%)
- DeepSWE 1.0 (provider harness): 62.0%
- SWE-bench Pro: **64.7%** — trails Opus 4.8 at 69.2% by 4.5 points, and Fable 5 at 80% by 15 points
- Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: #4 of 168 models (score 54)

**Token efficiency story:** Grok 4.5 uses ~15,954 output tokens per SWE-bench Pro task vs Opus 4.8 (max) at ~67,020 — 4.2x gap. If you're budget-constrained and willing to trade some agentic accuracy for throughput, this matters.

Musk described Grok 4.5 as "Opus-class." By xAI's own published numbers, Opus 4.8 leads on 2 of 4 tests at launch; all Opus 4.8 scores are independently verified by Artificial Analysis, Vals.ai, and BenchLM, while Grok 4.5 scores remain vendor-only.

### [NEW] Chinese AI models hit 46% of US enterprise token share on OpenRouter

CNBC July 7 reporting on OpenRouter data shows a structural shift in enterprise AI spending:

- Chinese models exceeded **30% of US enterprise token share on OpenRouter every week since February 8, 2026**, peaking at **46%**
- Prior 12-month average: **11%**
- DeepSeek V4 now holds **17.6%** of all OpenRouter-routed tokens (single largest vendor on platform)
- Alibaba Qwen: **13.9%** of OpenRouter tokens

**The cost math:** DeepSeek V4 Flash at $0.14/M input vs GPT-5.5 at $5.00/M — an order of magnitude gap. GLM-5.2 (Zhipu/Z.ai, launched June 2026) described by Jefferies as "almost equal to Anthropic as a competitor for the corporate market at one-quarter the cost per token."

**Named example:** Lindy (AI agent startup, ~25 employees) switched 100% of traffic from Anthropic Claude to DeepSeek. CEO Flo Crivello: "It's a matter of survival for the business. Expected to save the company millions of dollars within months." Open to returning to Claude if Anthropic cuts prices.

**Velocity indicator:** GLM-5.2 on Vercel tracked its fastest model adoption ever — daily token volume **27x** growth, customer count **80x** in its first full week. Morgan Stanley noted "demand mix is clearly shifting towards lower-cost models."

Analysts note Chinese models are estimated 6-9 months behind US frontier on quality. For production coding agents and similar high-stakes workloads, the quality gap still matters; for high-volume inference at the long tail, the cost gap is becoming hard to ignore.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic's IPO roadmap depends on maintaining enterprise revenue. The Fable 5 suspension (June 12 - July 1) gave enterprises 19 days to evaluate alternatives — and the CNBC data suggests many found Chinese options compelling on cost.
