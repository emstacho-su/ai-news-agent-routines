### [NEW] Anthropic Economic Scenario Explorer: GDP up 32%, knowledge-worker unemployment up to 17.9% in extreme case

Anthropic's Economics team released the Econ Scenario Explorer on September 9 — an interactive web tool projecting how AI adoption affects US GDP, wages, and unemployment through 2030. Users dial in task-automation share and replacement-vs-augmentation balance; the model outputs macro outcomes. Three reference scenarios:

- **Modest** (4% of tasks automated): +1.6% GDP, negligible employment impact.
- **Substantial** (12%, mostly replacement): +8.3% GDP, unemployment rises to 4.6%.
- **Extreme** (30%, mostly replacement): +32% GDP, overall unemployment 11.9%, knowledge-worker unemployment 17.9%, knowledge-worker wages down 11.5%, knowledge-worker employment down 21.5%.

Anthropic is explicit about limitations: the model omits policy responses, business cycles, financial market disruptions, and catastrophic-risk tail scenarios.

## Research & Technical

### [NEW] AlphaGenome Atlas: 9 billion DNA variant predictions, 1 petabyte, free for research

Google DeepMind published AlphaGenome Atlas on September 8 — a dataset covering every possible single-nucleotide variant across all ~9 billion positions in the human genome, plus 100M+ short insertions/deletions already observed in people. Each of the 9B variants carries ~27,000 individual predictions spanning hundreds of human and mouse cell/tissue types. Total size: 1 petabyte (30x larger than the AlphaFold Database). Comes with a new AVI variant-ranking score.

Access is free for academic research through a web portal (no coding required). Early external validation: Broad Institute and Exeter teams report a previously missed DNM1 disease variant surfaced, and 22% more non-coding genetic associations found in UK Biobank data. Not approved for clinical use.

Sources: [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-atlas-a-predictive-map-of-every-possible-dna-letter-change-in-the-human-genome/), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/new-google-deepmind-alphagenome-atlas-could-transform-our-understanding-of-genetic-diseases/), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02835-4)
