## Anthropic

### [UPDATE] Claude Code v2.1.158: Auto mode lands on Bedrock, Vertex, and Foundry

**Background:** Dynamic auto mode (intelligent model selection per task) shipped in Claude Code last month and has been available in claude.ai consumer tiers.

**New:** v2.1.158 (released May 30) extends auto mode to enterprise cloud deployments — AWS Bedrock, Google Vertex, and Microsoft Foundry — for both Opus 4.7 and Opus 4.8. Opt in by setting `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_AUTO_MODE=1`. Previously enterprise cloud users were locked out of auto mode regardless of model selection.

Sources: [GitHub Releases](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/releases), [Claude Code Changelog](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/changelog)

**Why it matters:** Bedrock/Vertex/Foundry are the dominant enterprise deployment paths. Auto mode reaching these platforms means teams that locked Claude Code behind corporate cloud controls can now benefit from automatic model-tier optimization without per-request configuration.

### [NEW] OpenAI Rosalind Biodefense Program: GPT-Rosalind access opened to vetted developers

OpenAI announced the Rosalind Biodefense Program on May 29, expanding trusted access to GPT-Rosalind — a life sciences model introduced in April that reasons about molecules, proteins, genes, and disease biology at a higher level than generic GPT models — to vetted developers and U.S. government partners. OpenAI covers access costs and provides launch support for applications in epidemiological modeling, early warning systems, diagnostics, and vaccine development. Early partners include Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, and CEPI.

## Research & Technical

### [NEW] Cisco: Multi-turn conversations raise AI jailbreak success rates 71%

Cisco published red-teaming research showing that no current frontier AI model is safe against iterative attacks. In multi-turn evaluations, attack success rates rose 71% after five conversational turns compared to single-turn baselines. The most extreme case: xAI Grok 4.1 Fast hit an 88.3% attack success rate in non-reasoning mode, dropping to 43.5% when reasoning was enabled — a configuration dependency that does not appear in xAI's official model card. The study's central finding is that published safety benchmarks are nearly all single-turn, meaning they systematically understate real-world vulnerability in the deployed conversational context where every production use of these models actually happens.

Sources: [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4177903/ai-models-more-vulnerable-than-claimed-when-faced-with-iterative-attacks.html), [Dataproof](https://www.dataproof.co.za/index.php/2026/05/27/ai-models-more-vulnerable-than-claimed-when-faced-with-iterative-attacks/)

## Wildcard

### [NEW] OpenAI Frontier Governance Framework: safety practices mapped to EU and California law

OpenAI published its Frontier Governance Framework on May 28 (announced/reported May 29). Rather than a new safety methodology, it is a public mapping of the existing Preparedness Framework onto specific regulatory obligations — the EU AI Act's Code of Practice for General Purpose AI and California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act. It covers risk assessment across four categories: cyber offense, CBRN risks, harmful manipulation, and loss of control. OpenAI describes using external domain experts and independent third-party evaluators who stress-test safeguards for models approaching a new risk tier.

Sources: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/openai-frontier-governance-framework/), [StartupHub.ai](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/artificial-intelligence/2026/openai-rolls-out-frontier-governance-framework)

## Practical tips

- Enterprise teams on Bedrock/Vertex/Foundry: `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_AUTO_MODE=1` in your shell environment or `.env` file activates the new auto mode for Opus 4.7 and 4.8 in v2.1.158+. Worth testing in a staging workspace before rolling out broadly.
- If you run red-team or security evaluations against AI models in your stack, treat single-turn safe-list assessments as a floor, not a ceiling — Cisco's data suggests five-turn adversarial conversation is a meaningful differentiator between models, and configuration (reasoning on vs. off) matters as much as model choice.
