## Tools & Frameworks

### [NEW] Nscale acquires Anyscale (Ray framework) for ~$1.65B

Nscale signed a definitive agreement July 30 to acquire Anyscale, the commercial steward of the Ray distributed computing framework, for approximately $1.65B. The deal positions Nscale as a vertically integrated AI cloud provider spanning power, data centers, GPU compute, and now software-layer workload orchestration. Anyscale will continue under its own brand and customers retain freedom to run workloads on any infrastructure. The Ray open-source project was already donated to the PyTorch Foundation in 2025 and remains community-governed independent of this acquisition. Transaction expected to close H2 2026 pending regulatory clearance.

**Why it matters:** Ray is the dominant distributed training/inference orchestration layer for many production ML stacks; the deal changes the long-term roadmap incentive for Ray's enterprise features and support.

## Model Releases

### [UPDATE] GPT-5.6: Luna drops 80%, Terra drops 20%

**Background:** GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna launched publicly July 9 at Sol $5/$30, Terra $2.50/$15, Luna $1/$6 per Mtok.

**New:** Effective July 30, Luna drops 80% to **$0.20/$1.20 per Mtok** (input/output) and Terra drops 20% to $2/$12 per Mtok. Speculative decoding improvements delivered 15%+ better token-generation efficiency across the family. Auto-review in ChatGPT and Codex CLI was simultaneously upgraded to GPT-5.6 Luna.

**Why it matters:** $0.20 input is cheaper than Claude Haiku 4.5 ($0.80 input). If Luna quality holds for agentic review and routing tasks, this reshapes cost tradeoffs for high-volume pipelines currently on the cheapest Claude or Gemini Flash tier.

Sources: [Radical Data Science July Bulletin](https://radicaldatascience.wordpress.com/2026/07/30/ai-news-briefs-bulletin-board-for-july-2026/comment-page-1/), [Releasebot OpenAI](https://releasebot.io/updates/openai)

### [NEW] Gemini Robotics 2: whole-body intelligence for humanoid robots

Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2 on July 30, introducing "whole-body intelligence" — AI control that coordinates full-body motion, balance, and navigation for humanoid robots, expanding beyond prior upper-body-only systems. Ships as three specialized models: a Vision-Language-Action model, an Embodied Reasoning model, and an On-Device controller for humanoid platforms. Benchmarks on the Apollo 2 robot: 68.4% success picking objects from a table, 45.7% from the floor, 76.3% from a shelf. The system generalizes to tasks it was not explicitly trained on and adapts to new two-arm robot designs with fewer than 200 training examples.

## Wildcard

### [UPDATE] TRAINS August 1: framework pending, Altman concedes AI may need to slow

**Background:** Trump's June EO gave federal agencies 60 days (until August 1) to produce a pre-release AI review framework. TRAINS (Testing Risks of AI for National Security), run through Commerce/CAISI, gives the government up to 30 days of pre-release access to frontier models above 10^25 training FLOPs. Confirmed participants: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, xAI (Amazon reportedly also participating). Meta was the sole holdout under direct White House pressure.

**New (July 29-30):** The formal framework had not been publicly released as of the latest reporting (July 29-30) and was described as "days from announcement." Sam Altman met White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles and then visited the Senate July 29-30, where he made a significant public statement: **"we may have to pace the rate of AI development to give ourselves enough time for society to harden around some of these new capability levels"** — his first public acknowledgment of a potential need to slow down. Meta's position clarified structurally: Llama open-weight models are publicly downloadable after release, making a closed-API pre-release window architecturally incompatible without a fundamentally different framework for open-weight models; the White House acknowledged it "hopes to sign the agreement soon" but no agreement reached. Conflict-of-interest concern unchanged: OpenAI and Anthropic are co-drafting the evaluation thresholds competitors must clear, with no announced plan for independent input.

Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/altman-white-house-wiles-ai-framework.html), [Eastern Herald](https://easternherald.com/2026/07/30/sam-altman-senate-ai-pacing-white-house-deadline-openai/), [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321497/20260724/voluntary-paper-mandatory-practice-white-house-ai-review-hits-august-1-deadline.htm), [Digitimes](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260729VL219/government-anthropic-openai-licensing-cybersecurity.html), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/we-hope-to-sign-the-agreement-soon-white-house-calls-on-meta-to-submit-ai-models-for-review-citing-abilities-and-vulnerabilities-evaluation)

### [NEW] EU AI Gigafactory tender: €30B, 7 sites, applications close November 12

The European Commission opened its AI Gigafactory call for tenders on July 30, committing up to €10B in EU and national public funding and targeting an additional €20B in private co-investment across seven planned sites. Two funding lots: Lot 1 (up to €100M rising to €400M per site in phase two) and Lot 2 (up to €200M rising to €800M). Application deadline November 12, 2026; award decisions expected early 2027. The sites are intended to provide EU startups, SMEs, and researchers access to frontier-scale compute for training, inference, and fine-tuning — Europe's response to falling behind on AI compute infrastructure.
